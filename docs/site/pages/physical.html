Physical design

Tiqora-Serial has a clean layout at 1x2, the size info.yaml submits. The Tiny Tapeout gds, pre-flight and gate-level workflows pass on GitHub, and that run's own report gives the numbers in the table on this page (run 40624495043, src/ as on main 168fc84). It meets setup and hold timing at 50 MHz at all nine corners. No chip exists yet.

What it is good for

At 130 nm, today

Giving a PC or Raspberry Pi nanosecond timing it cannot get from its own pins: a USB time-interval meter, event logging with ordering across 8 inputs, and exact-tick triggers for cameras, lasers or data acquisition. Not for reacting to an event faster than a serial round trip; the Tiqora microcontroller does that on chip.

On a smaller process

A faster clock gives finer timestamps (resolution is one clock period), and the shared timing unit, already CPU-independent, fits a larger system-on-chip as a timestamp-and-trigger peripheral behind a faster host link than the UART. None of this is measured.

Ready to move

No process-specific cells in the RTL, no latches, no macros, synchronous reset, one clock. The sky130 parts live in src/config.json, the cell lists and the Tiny Tapeout wrapper. Details: docs/APPLICATIONS.md; references: docs/LIBRARY.md.
